Vinegar math

The bottle says 10%, the recipe says 5%, and the crock of leftover wine says nothing at all. Acidity is one multiplication - worth running before you cook with it.

Run the acid before you cook

The problem it solves

Dilution trips everyone because the water goes in the denominator too: cutting 500 ml of 10% vinegar to 5% is not 500 ml of water thinking - it is, but only because the acid mass stays put while the volume doubles. Get it wrong by instinct and the dressing lands somewhere you never measured.

Making your own is the same arithmetic running forward: alcohol converts to acetic acid at roughly one to one, so a 12% wine ends near 12% acidity - far too strong for the salad without a cut. Vinegar math runs both directions, plus the batch check for the jug you already mixed: dilute a strong bottle to a target, plan a crock from the wine you have, and check the final acidity of whatever is already in the jug.

What it does not do

Vinegar math multiplies; it does not measure. No calculation here can tell you the real acidity of a crock, and no output on this site says whether a preserve is safe. Do not use homemade or ABV-estimated vinegar for pickling or canning: preservation calls for a tested recipe with commercially produced vinegar of a stated acidity, as the National Center for Home Food Preservation and Clemson Extension require.
